What Is Surgical Technology?
Allied health profession
Surgical technology is a specialized allied health profession focused on patient care in the surgical setting
Perioperative care
STs work across all three perioperative phases: preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative
Sterile technique
Central competency: maintaining asepsis and the sterile field to prevent surgical site infections
Textbook: Surgical Technology: Principles and Practice, 9th Edition — John Ratliff. This chapter covers the profession's history, organizations, roles, ethics, and career paths.

Historical Evolution of Surgical Technology
Pre
WWII
Before World War II
Nurses served as surgical assistants. Primary duties included instrument preparation and assisting with ether (anesthesia) administration. No distinct "surgical technologist" role existed.
Post
WWII
After World War II
Demand for OR personnel dramatically increased. A critical nursing shortage emerged. Military corpsmen were trained to assist in surgery, filling the staffing gap and laying the groundwork for a new profession.
Korean
War
Korean War Era
Nursing shortage continued. Roles shifted: corpsmen began circulating while nurses scrubbed. The Association of Operating Room Technicians (AORT) was developed. Formal training programs began.
1968
1968 — AORN Creates AORT
The Association of Operating Room Nurses (AORN) officially created the Association of Operating Room Technicians (AORT). The Liaison Council on Certification for the Surgical Technologist (LCC-ST) and the Joint Review Committee on Education were also established.
1970
1970 — First Certifying Exam
The first certifying examination for surgical technologists was administered, formalizing professional competency standards.
1973
1973 — AORT Becomes AST
The AORT was renamed the Association of Surgical Technologists (AST). "Certified technician" became the Certified Surgical Technologist (CST) — the credential that remains in use today.
Exam tip: The sequence matters — AORT (1968) → First exam (1970) → AORT renamed AST (1973) → CST credential created. Know which war triggered each phase: WWII = nursing shortage, Korean War = formal training began.
Scrub Role — STSR (Sterile Team)
Scrubbed, gowned, and gloved. Delivers direct surgical care as part of the sterile team.
Sterile field
Establishes, prepares, and maintains the sterile field throughout surgery
Instrument passing
Prepares and passes instruments, supplies, medications, and equipment to the surgeon during the procedure
Field protection
Protects and maintains the integrity of the sterile field at all times
Circulator Role (Non-Sterile Team)
Non-sterile team member. Shares some but not all RN responsibilities — varies by state scope of practice.
Equipment
Delivers and prepares non-sterile equipment for use in the OR
Supply opening
Distributes and opens sterile supplies to sterile team members using aseptic technique
Patient care
Assists with patient transfer and positioning; performs urinary catheterization; performs surgical skin prep
Other Perioperative Responsibilities
Emergency duty
On-call response to urgent/emergent surgical cases outside routine hours
Instrument preparation
Prepare, organize, and care for surgical instruments and specialized equipment
Leadership & management
Oversight, coordination, and management duties within the surgical department
Preceptor
Educate and guide students and new staff during clinical training rotations

Professional Organizations & Accrediting Bodies
AST — Association of Surgical Technologists Primary
The main professional organization for surgical technologists (formed 1973, renamed from AORT). Publishes the Code of Ethics, Standards of Practice, and Code of Conduct for the profession.
NBSTSA — National Board of Surgical Technology and Surgical Assisting Certifying
The certifying body responsible for administering the CST exam. Candidates must graduate from an accredited surgical technology program before sitting for the exam.
ARC/STSA — Accreditation Review Council on Education in Surgical Technology and Surgical Assisting Accrediting
Recommends accreditation of surgical technology education programs. Works alongside CAAHEP to ensure programs meet quality standards.
CAAHEP — Commission on Accreditation of Allied Health Education Programs Accrediting
Grants program accreditation to allied health education programs based on ARC/STSA recommendations. Programs must be CAAHEP- or ABHES-accredited for graduates to sit the CST exam.
ABHES — Accrediting Bureau of Health Education Schools Accrediting
Nonprofit accrediting body recognized by the U.S. Department of Education. An alternative pathway to program accreditation alongside CAAHEP.
AORT — Association of Operating Room Technicians Historical
Created in 1968 by AORN. Became the AST in 1973. The predecessor to today's AST.
Education Pathway
Step 1: Complete an accredited surgical technology program (ABHES or ARC/STSA accredited)
Step 2: Institutional accreditation from U.S. Dept. of Education or The Joint Commission
Step 3: Graduate and sit for the CST exam (NBSTSA)
Step 4: Earn CST credential; maintain with 30 CE credits per 2-year cycle
Accreditation Relationship
ARC/STSA → CAAHEP
ARC/STSA recommends; CAAHEP grants accreditation to the program
ABHES (alternative)
Separate accrediting body; recognized by U.S. Dept. of Education; also qualifies graduates for CST exam
Why it matters
Only graduates of CAAHEP- or ABHES-accredited programs are eligible to sit for the NBSTSA CST examination
Characteristics of a Professional
Integrity — consistently honest and ethical; acts with moral principles even when not observed
Self-regulation — monitors and manages one's own behavior without external enforcement
Truthfulness — communicates honestly with patients, colleagues, and supervisors
Commitment — dedicated to patient safety and professional excellence
Politeness — respectful in all professional interactions
Tact and discretion — handles sensitive situations and confidential information appropriately
Respect for rules, regulations, and laws — follows institutional policies and legal requirements
Perspective — maintains appropriate professional viewpoint; avoids personal bias in patient care
Professional Ethics for Health Care Workers
Respect human individuality and uniqueness — treat every patient as a unique individual
Practice your profession with empathy — understand patients' experiences and emotions
Practice equal rights — provide equitable care regardless of background, identity, or status
Respect all confidences entrusted to you — maintain patient privacy (HIPAA)
Respect the free will of the patient — honor patient autonomy and informed consent
Do no harm (non-maleficence) — avoid actions that may injure or endanger the patient
Surgical conscience — personal commitment to sterile technique and patient safety, even when no one is watching
Surgical conscience is the cornerstone concept in ST ethics: the internal standard that drives the technologist to always maintain sterile technique and immediately report breaks in sterility, regardless of who might observe.
Ethical Challenges & Dilemmas
Impairment in the workplace
Healthcare professionals have both a legal and ethical responsibility to report suspected impairment (substance use, fatigue, illness) of a coworker, or to seek treatment themselves. Patient safety is paramount.
Refusal to perform an assigned task
May occur due to ethical, religious, or moral objections. Must be handled according to institutional policy; patient care must never be abandoned.
Refusal to work with a specific individual
Personal conflicts in the OR require professional resolution. Patient care takes priority over interpersonal disagreements.
Ethical dilemma: A personal conflict arising from the need to make a decision based on choices that are NOT completely acceptable — no "perfect" answer exists. The AST Code of Ethics guides decision-making in these situations.

Focus in a surgical specialty (cardiac, neuro, ortho, plastics, trauma)

Advanced role; provides direct patient care during surgery; requires additional certification
📚
Educator & Clinical Instructor
Teach in accredited surgical technology programs; clinical preceptorship
💼
Medical Industry Representative
Sales and technical support for surgical instruments, equipment, implants, or devices
⚙️
Central Processing Management
Manage sterile processing departments; instrument reprocessing and sterility assurance
🔬
Research, Product & Development
Contribute clinical expertise to development and testing of new surgical technologies
